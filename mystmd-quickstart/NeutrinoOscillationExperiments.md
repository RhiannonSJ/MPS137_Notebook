# Introduction to Neutrino Oscillations & Sources

Neutrino oscillation is a quantum mechanical phenomenon where a neutrino created with a specific lepton flavour ($\nu_e, \nu_\mu, \nu_\tau$) is later measured to have a different flavour[cite: 1]. Experiments probe these quantum transitions using two primary, complementary measurement strategies. Appearance searches look for a specific neutrino flavour in the detector that was not present in the initial beam or source, such as detecting $\nu_e$ from an initial $\nu_\mu$ beam[cite: 1]. These offer exceptional sensitivity to small mixing angles and CP-violating phases because background levels from intrinsic flavour contamination are generally low[cite: 1]. Conversely, disappearance searches measure the survival probability of the initial neutrino flavour, such as tracking $\bar{\nu}_e \to \bar{\nu}_e$ or $\nu_\mu \to \nu_\mu$[cite: 1]. By mapping the deficit in expected flux as a function of energy and baseline distance, disappearance channels provide direct, high-precision constraints on mass-squared splittings and mixing angles[cite: 1].

To probe these oscillations across vastly different baselines ($L$) and energies ($E$), experimentalists utilize diverse terrestrial and natural neutrino sources[cite: 1]. Accelerator neutrinos are produced artificially by colliding high-energy protons into heavy targets like graphite or beryllium to generate pions and kaons, which subsequently decay into directed, high-purity beams of $\nu_\mu$ or $\bar{\nu}_\mu$ in the MeV to GeV energy range[cite: 1]. Reactor neutrinos serve as intense, terrestrial sources of pure electron antineutrinos ($\bar{\nu}_e$) emitted isotropically via nuclear fission of $^{235}\text{U}$, $^{238}\text{U}$, $^{239}\text{Pu}$, and $^{241}\text{Pu}$ at low energy scales between $1\text{ MeV}$ and $8\text{ MeV}$[cite: 1].

Natural sources provide complementary energy regimes and baselines[cite: 1]. Solar neutrinos are low-energy $\nu_e$ ($< 20\text{ MeV}$) produced via nuclear fusion chains in the Sun's core, providing essential insights into matter-enhanced oscillation mechanisms known as the MSW effect[cite: 1]. Atmospheric neutrinos span a broad energy range from MeV to TeV scales and are generated when high-energy cosmic rays strike Earth's upper atmosphere, producing hadronic showers that decay into $\nu_e$ and $\nu_\mu$ with baselines ranging from $10\text{ km}$ overhead to $13,000\text{ km}$ through the Earth's core[cite: 1]. Finally, astrophysical sources yield extreme fluxes: cosmogenic neutrinos are produced at ultra-high energies (PeV to EeV) when cosmic rays interact with the Cosmic Microwave Background via the GZK effect, while extragalactic neutrinos (TeV to PeV) originate from high-energy cosmic accelerators such as Active Galactic Nuclei, blazars, and Gamma-Ray Bursts beyond the Milky Way[cite: 1].

The following sections summarize the current, legacy, and next-generation suite of neutrino oscillation experiments globally, categorized by primary source and baseline[cite: 1].

# Long-Baseline Accelerator Experiments

## T2K (Tokai to Kamioka)

* **Location:** Japan (Beam: J-PARC in Tokai; Far Detector: Kamioka Observatory, Gifu)
* **Baseline ($L$):** $295\text{ km}$ (Far Detector); $280\text{ m}$ (Near Detector ND280)[^1]
* **Detector Technology:**
  * **Near Detectors (ND280, INGRID):** Plastic scintillators, TPCs, and fine-grained detectors inside a magnet.
  * **Far Detector (Super-Kamiokande):** A 50 kt water Cherenkov detector viewed by over 11,000 PMTs.
* **Timeline:** Started 2010; currently running (Phase-II upgrades ongoing).
* **Neutrino Source:** Accelerator (an off-axis $1.6^\circ$ narrow-band $\nu_\mu / \bar{\nu}_\mu$ beam generated by 30 GeV protons hitting a graphite target).
* **Primary Flavour:** $\nu_\mu / \bar{\nu}_\mu$
* **Energy Range:** Peak energy $E_\nu \sim 0.6\text{ GeV}$
* **Primary Oscillation Parameters:** Mixing angle $\theta_{23}$, mass-squared difference $|\Delta m^2_{32}|$, and the CP-violating phase $\delta_{\text{CP}}$.
* **Dominant Systematics & Backgrounds:**
  * **Systematics:** Complex neutrino-nucleus interaction modelling (such as multinucleon $2p2h$ processes) and flux normalization.
  * **Backgrounds:** Neutral current events with a single pion ($\text{NC}1\pi^0$) where photon showers mimic single electron tracks from $\nu_e$ interactions.
* **Secondary Physics Goals:** Neutrino-nucleus cross-section measurements, sterile neutrino searches, and non-standard interactions.
* **Fun Fact:** The beamline targets a massive underground water tank situated 295 km away. To line up the beam with pinpoint accuracy, geodetic survey techniques were refined using GPS and satellite data, pointing the beam directly **through** the Earth's crust so that it grazes the detector underground![cite: 1]

[^1]: K. Abe et al. (T2K Collaboration), "Indication of Electron Neutrino Appearance from an Accelerator-produced Muon Neutrino Beam," Phys. Rev. Lett. 107, 041801 (2011).[cite: 1]

## NOvA (NuMI Off-axis $\nu_e$ Appearance)

* **Location:** United States (Beam/Near Detector: Fermilab, Batavia, IL; Far Detector: Ash River, MN)
* **Baseline ($L$):** $810\text{ km}$ (Far Detector); $1\text{ km}$ (Near Detector)[^2]
* **Detector Technology:** Extruded PVC cells filled with liquid scintillator, read out using wavelength-shifting fibres connected to Avalanche Photodiodes (APDs).
* **Timeline:** Started 2014; currently running.
* **Neutrino Source:** Accelerator (NuMI off-axis $14\text{ mrad}$ beam produced by 120 GeV protons from the Main Injector).
* **Primary Flavour:** $\nu_\mu / \bar{\nu}_\mu$
* **Energy Range:** Peak energy $E_\nu \sim 2.0\text{ GeV}$
* **Primary Oscillation Parameters:** Mass Ordering (Normal vs. Inverted via Earth matter effects), $\theta_{23}$ octant, and $\delta_{\text{CP}}$.
* **Dominant Systematics & Backgrounds:**
  * **Systematics:** Absolute light level calibration, detector energy scale, and cross-section modelling.
  * **Backgrounds:** Unshielded cosmic-ray muons (due to the detector's location on the surface), intrinsic beam $\nu_e$, and neutral current events producing neutral pions.
* **Secondary Physics Goals:** Core-collapse supernova neutrino detection, atmospheric neutrino monitoring, cosmic-ray physics, and searches for magnetic monopoles.
* **Fun Fact:** Because the Far Detector sits on the surface without the shielding of a deep cave, it gets blasted by roughly 150,000 cosmic rays **per second**. To spot a handful of neutrino interactions a day amidst that noise, the detector uses ultra-precise timing synchronization down to nanoseconds![cite: 1]

[^2]: M. A. Acero et al. (NOvA Collaboration), "First Measurement of Neutrino Oscillation Parameters using Neutrinos and Antineutrinos by NOvA," Phys. Rev. Lett. 123, 151801 (2019).[cite: 1]

## DUNE (Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment)

* **Location:** United States (Beam/Near Detector: Fermilab, IL; Far Detector: SURF, Lead, SD)
* **Baseline ($L$):** $1,300\text{ km}$ (Far Detector); $574\text{ m}$ (Near Detector)[^3]
* **Detector Technology:**
  * **Near Detector:** Multi-component system (Liquid Argon TPC, SAND detector, and mobile ND-GAr).
  * **Far Detector:** Four 17 kt modules using Liquid Argon Time Projection Chamber (LArTPC) technology, located 1,480 m underground.
* **Timeline:** Construction ongoing; first data expected in the late 2020s.
* **Neutrino Source:** Accelerator (LBNF broadband beam generated by 60–120 GeV protons).
* **Primary Flavour:** $\nu_\mu / \bar{\nu}_\mu$
* **Energy Range:** Broadband $0.5\text{ GeV} - 5.0\text{ GeV}$
* **Primary Oscillation Parameters:** Unambiguous discovery of CP violation ($\delta_{\text{CP}}$) across a broad spectrum, Mass Ordering ($>5\sigma$), $\theta_{23}$, and $\Delta m^2_{31}$.
* **Dominant Systematics & Backgrounds:**
  * **Systematics:** LArTPC absolute energy scale calibration, shower reconstruction efficiency, and Final State Interactions (FSI) inside the argon nucleus.
  * **Backgrounds:** Intrinsic beam $\nu_e$ contamination and misidentified neutral current events with high-momentum neutral pions.
* **Secondary Physics Goals:** Searches for proton decay (specifically $\text{p} \to \text{K}^+ \bar{\nu}$), core-collapse supernova electron-neutrino bursts, and BSM physics (e.g., light dark matter, heavy neutral leptons).
* **Fun Fact:** The Far Detector is taking over the historic underground cavity where Ray Davis conducted his Nobel Prize-winning Homestake solar neutrino experiment in the late 1960s—where the original "Solar Neutrino Problem" was born![cite: 1]

[^3]: B. Abi et al. (DUNE Collaboration), "Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment (DUNE), Far Detector Technical Design Report, Volume I," JINST 15, T08008 (2020).[cite: 1]

# Short-Baseline Accelerator Experiments (eV-Scale Sterile Searches)

## MicroBooNE & SBN Program (Short-Baseline Neutrino)

* **Location:** United States (Fermilab, Batavia, IL)
* **Baseline ($L$):** $110\text{ m}$ (SBND), $470\text{ m}$ (MicroBooNE), $600\text{ m}$ (ICARUS)[^4]
* **Detector Technology:** Liquid Argon Time Projection Chambers (LArTPCs). The SBN program comprises three detectors along the same beamline:
  * **SBND (Near Detector, 110 m):** 112-ton active LArTPC.
  * **MicroBooNE (Intermediate, 470 m):** 85-ton active LArTPC.
  * **ICARUS (Far Detector, 600 m):** 476-ton active LArTPC (relocated from LNGS, Italy).
* **Timeline:** MicroBooNE ran 2015–2021; ICARUS began taking data in 2021; SBND began physics data collection in late 2024. Program currently running.
* **Neutrino Source:** Accelerator (Booster Neutrino Beam [BNB] generated by 8 GeV protons hitting a beryllium target).
* **Primary Flavour:** $\nu_\mu$ (with search for $\nu_e$ appearance and $\nu_\mu$ disappearance)
* **Energy Range:** Peak energy $E_\nu \sim 0.7\text{ GeV} - 0.8\text{ GeV}$
* **Primary Oscillation Parameters:** Short-baseline oscillations driven by an eV-scale mass splitting ($\Delta m^2_{41} \sim 1\text{ eV}^2$), searching for sterile neutrino mixing ($\theta_{14}, \theta_{24}$).
* **Dominant Systematics & Backgrounds:**
  * **Systematics:** Flux shape uncertainties, electron-photon discrimination in LAr, and low-energy neutrino-argon interaction cross sections.
  * **Backgrounds:** Single-photon shower events ($\text{NC}\Delta \to N\gamma$), intrinsic beam $\nu_e$, and cosmic rays entering the shallow surface-level detectors.
* **Secondary Physics Goals:** High-statistics measurement of low-energy neutrino-argon cross sections, Dark Sector particle searches (light dark matter, heavy neutral leptons), and detector technology validation for DUNE.
* **Fun Fact:** The SBND oscillation working group and detector development involve major key contributions from researchers at the University of Sheffield![cite: 1]

[^4]: P. A. Machado et al., "The Short-Baseline Neutrino Program at Fermilab," arXiv:1903.04608 (2019).[cite: 1]

## MiniBooNE (Legacy)

* **Location:** United States (Fermilab, Batavia, IL)
* **Baseline ($L$):** $541\text{ m}$[^5]
* **Detector Technology:** 800-ton pure mineral oil Cherenkov detector equipped with 1,280 PMTs.
* **Timeline:** 2002 – 2019 (Completed).
* **Neutrino Source:** Accelerator (Booster Neutrino Beam at 541 m baseline).
* **Primary Flavour:** $\nu_\mu / \bar{\nu}_\mu$
* **Energy Range:** $0.2\text{ GeV} - 1.25\text{ GeV}$
* **Primary Oscillation Parameters:** Short-baseline $\nu_\mu \to \nu_e$ appearance ($\Delta m^2 \sim 0.1 - 10\text{ eV}^2$).
* **Dominant Systematics & Backgrounds:**
  * **Systematics:** Beam flux predictions and misidentified neutral-current pion production.
  * **Backgrounds:** Neutral-current $\pi^0$ events where one photon was lost, mimicking electron Cherenkov rings.
* **Secondary Physics Goals:** Cross-section measurements on carbon targets, dark matter searches in beam-dump mode.
* **Fun Fact:** MiniBooNE ran for nearly two decades and accumulated a massive, puzzling $4.8\sigma$ excess of electron-like events that confounded theorists for years, driving the development of the entire modern short-baseline liquid argon research program![cite: 1]

[^5]: A. A. Aguilar-Arevalo et al. (MiniBooNE Collaboration), "Significant Excess of Electronlike Events in the MiniBooNE Short-Baseline Neutrino Experiment," Phys. Rev. Lett. 121, 221801 (2018).[cite: 1]

# Reactor Neutrino Experiments

## Daya Bay

* **Location:** China (Daya Bay/Ling Ao Nuclear Power Plant, Shenzhen)
* **Baseline ($L$):** $360\text{ m} - 500\text{ m}$ (Near Hall detectors); $1.5\text{ km} - 1.9\text{ km}$ (Far Hall detectors)[^6]
* **Detector Technology:** Gadolinium-doped Liquid Scintillator (Gd-LS) nested in an un-doped LS buffer inside PMT-instrumented tanks submerged in water pools.
* **Timeline:** 2011 – 2020 (Completed).
* **Neutrino Source:** Commercial nuclear reactors ($6 \times 2.9\text{ GW}_{\text{th}}$ cores).
* **Primary Flavour:** Electron antineutrinos ($\bar{\nu}_e$)
* **Energy Range:** $1.0\text{ MeV} - 8.0\text{ MeV}$
* **Primary Oscillation Parameters:** Precision measurement of mixing angle $\theta_{13}$ and effective mass-squared splitting $|\Delta m^2_{ee}|$.
* **Dominant Systematics & Backgrounds:**
  * **Systematics:** Target mass uncertainty across identical modules and reactor flux model uncertainties (including the infamous "5 MeV bump").
  * **Backgrounds:** Cosmogenic isotopes ($^9\text{Li}/^8\text{He}$) and fast neutrons from cosmic-ray spallation.
* **Secondary Physics Goals:** Precision reactor antineutrino spectrum and flux mapping, light sterile neutrino searches (4th mass state).
* **Fun Fact:** The experiment was a literal race against time. Daya Bay was built to catch the then-unknown mixing angle $\theta_{13}$. They announced a non-zero measurement in 2012 with $5.2\sigma$ significance, beating international competitors to the punch and revealing that $\theta_{13}$ was surprisingly large![cite: 1]

[^6]: F. P. An et al. (Daya Bay Collaboration), "Observation of Electron-Antineutrino Disappearance at Daya Bay," Phys. Rev. Lett. 108, 171803 (2012).[cite: 1]

## JUNO (Jiangmen Underground Neutrino Observatory)

* **Location:** China (Kaiping, Jiangmen, Guangdong)
* **Baseline ($L$):** $52.5\text{ km}$ (equalized baseline to both the Yangjiang and Taishan Nuclear Power Plants)[^7]
* **Detector Technology:** A 20 kt liquid scintillator sphere enclosed in a giant 35.4 m acrylic vessel, monitored by over 20,000 20-inch PMTs inside a 44 m water pool.
* **Timeline:** Began collecting data in August 2025; currently running.[^8]
* **Neutrino Source:** Yangjiang and Taishan Nuclear Power Plants (at an optimized 53 km baseline).
* **Primary Flavour:** Electron antineutrinos ($\bar{\nu}_e$)
* **Energy Range:** $1.0\text{ MeV} - 8.0\text{ MeV}$
* **Primary Oscillation Parameters:** Mass Ordering via sub-percent energy resolution ($\le 3\% \text{ at } 1\text{ MeV}$), and ultra-precise measurement of $\sin^2\theta_{12}$, $\Delta m^2_{21}$, and $|\Delta m^2_{32}|$.
* **Dominant Systematics & Backgrounds:**
  * **Systematics:** Non-linearity of the energy response, optical transparency of the liquid scintillator over long paths, and reactor flux variations.
  * **Backgrounds:** Cosmogenic $^9\text{Li}/^8\text{He}$, $(\alpha, n)$ radioactive reactions from surrounding material, and background geoneutrinos.
* **Secondary Physics Goals:** Geoneutrinos from Earth's crust/mantle, Diffuse Supernova Neutrino Background (DSNB), solar neutrinos, and atmospheric neutrinos.
* **Fun Fact:** The central sphere holds 20,000 tons of liquid scintillator inside an acrylic shell just 12 cm thick—making it the largest single acrylic structure ever built in human history![cite: 1]

[^7]: F. An et al. (JUNO Collaboration), "Neutrino Physics with JUNO," J. Phys. G: Nucl. Part. Phys. 43, 030401 (2016).[cite: 1]
[^8]: JUNO Collaboration, "First Measurement of Reactor Antineutrino Spectrum and Oscillation Parameters at JUNO," Nature (2026).[cite: 1]

# Natural & Atmospheric Source Experiments

## Super-Kamiokande (SK)

* **Location:** Japan (Kamioka Observatory, Hida, Gifu)
* **Baseline ($L$):** Solar: $1.5 \times 10^8\text{ km}$; Atmospheric: $10\text{ km} - 13,000\text{ km}$ (variable path length through Earth)[^9]
* **Detector Technology:** 50 kt ultra-pure water Cherenkov detector (currently in the SK-Gd phase with dissolved Gadolinium sulphate).
* **Timeline:** Started 1996; currently running.
* **Neutrino Source:** Cosmic rays interacting in the atmosphere, and solar fusion reactions ($^8\text{B}$, $\text{hep}$).
* **Primary Flavour:** $\nu_e, \bar{\nu}_e, \nu_\mu, \bar{\nu}_\mu$
* **Energy Range:** Solar: $3.5\text{ MeV} - 20\text{ MeV}$; Atmospheric: $100\text{ MeV}$ to TeV scale
* **Primary Oscillation Parameters:** Atmospheric $\theta_{23}$ and $|\Delta m^2_{32}|$, solar $\theta_{12}$ and $\Delta m^2_{21}$ (via day/night matter effects).
* **Dominant Systematics & Backgrounds:**
  * **Systematics:** 3D atmospheric flux modelling uncertainties and primary hadron interaction models.
  * **Backgrounds:** Radon ($^{222}\text{Rn}$) decays in water at low energies, and neutral current atmospheric interactions.
* **Secondary Physics Goals:** Proton decay searches ($\text{p} \to e^+ \pi^0$), galactic core-collapse supernova warnings, DSNB, and dark matter searches.
* **Fun Fact:** The water inside Super-K is so ultra-pure that it acts as a harsh solvent; it can slowly leach ions out of normal metals and plastics, and scientists must navigate the tank in rubber dinghies during rare maintenance periods to keep from contaminating it![cite: 1]

[^9]: Y. Fukuda et al. (Super-Kamiokande Collaboration), "Evidence for Oscillation of Atmospheric Neutrinos," Phys. Rev. Lett. 81, 1562 (1998); K. Abe et al., "Solar Neutrino Measurements in Super-Kamiokande-IV," Phys. Rev. D 94, 052010 (2016).[cite: 1]

## Hyper-Kamiokande (Hyper-K)

* **Location:** Japan (Kamioka, Hida, Gifu; J-PARC beamline from Tokai)
* **Baseline ($L$):** Accelerator Beam: $295\text{ km}$; Solar: $1.5 \times 10^8\text{ km}$; Atmospheric: $10\text{ km} - 13,000\text{ km}$[^10]
* **Detector Technology:** Next-generation subterranean water Cherenkov detector containing 258 kt of ultra-pure water (190 kt fiducial mass)—over 8 times the fiducial volume of Super-Kamiokande—instrumented with ultra-high-sensitivity PMTs.
* **Timeline:** Construction ongoing; planned operations start in late 2027 or 2028.
* **Neutrino Source:** Upgraded high-intensity J-PARC accelerator narrow-band beam ($1.3\text{ MW}$ proton beam), along with atmospheric and solar neutrinos.
* **Primary Flavour:** $\nu_\mu, \bar{\nu}_\mu, \nu_e, \bar{\nu}_e$
* **Energy Range:** Solar: $>2\text{ MeV}$; Beam/Atmospheric: $100\text{ MeV} - \text{tens of GeV}$
* **Primary Oscillation Parameters:** High-precision determination of leptonic CP violation ($\delta_{\text{CP}}$) covering over $60\%$ of parameter space at $>5\sigma$, precise mixing angle $\theta_{23}$ octant, and mass squared differences.
* **Dominant Systematics & Backgrounds:**
  * **Systematics:** Neutrino interaction cross-section models, beam flux uncertainties, and PMT timing/photosensor calibration.
  * **Backgrounds:** Atmospheric $\text{NC}1\pi^0$ interactions mimicking $\nu_e$ single-ring events, and cosmic-ray spallation products.
* **Secondary Physics Goals:** Unprecedented sensitivity to proton decay ($\text{p} \to e^+ \pi^0$ lifetime sensitivity exceeding $10^{35}$ years), galactic supernova burst detection (expecting tens of thousands of events within seconds), solar neutrino day-night asymmetry, and DSNB discovery.
* **Fun Fact:** Hyper-K's main underground cavern is so colossal (60 metres in diameter and 71 metres tall) that it could easily fit the entire United States Capitol building inside it![cite: 1]

[^10]: K. Abe et al. (Hyper-Kamiokande Proto-Collaboration), "Hyper-Kamiokande Design Report," arXiv:1805.04163 (2018).[cite: 1]

## IceCube / DeepCore

* **Location:** Amundsen-Scott South Pole Station, Antarctica
* **Baseline ($L$):** Atmospheric: $20\text{ km} - 12,700\text{ km}$ (depending on zenith angle through Earth); Astrophysical: $> 10^{19}\text{ km}$[^11]
* **Detector Technology:** A $1\text{ km}^3$ array of Digital Optical Modules (DOMs) melted deep into the Antarctic ice cap; DeepCore is a densely instrumented sub-array at its center.
* **Timeline:** Started 2010; currently running.
* **Neutrino Source:** High-energy atmospheric cosmic ray air showers.
* **Primary Flavour:** $\nu_\mu, \bar{\nu}_\mu, \nu_e, \bar{\nu}_e, \nu_\tau, \bar{\nu}_\tau$
* **Energy Range:** DeepCore: $5\text{ GeV} - 100\text{ GeV}$; IceCube Main Array: $>100\text{ GeV}$ to PeV scale
* **Primary Oscillation Parameters:** Atmospheric mixing parameters $\theta_{23}$ and $|\Delta m^2_{32}|$, and tau neutrino ($\nu_\tau$) appearance.
* **Dominant Systematics & Backgrounds:**
  * **Systematics:** Optical properties of deep glacial ice (light scattering, absorption, and directional anisotropy caused by dust layers from prehistoric volcanic eruptions), and DOM angular sensitivity.
  * **Backgrounds:** Downward-going cosmic-ray atmospheric muons.
* **Secondary Physics Goals:** High-energy neutrino astrophysics (blazars, GRBs), cosmic-ray anisotropy, and searches for sterile neutrinos.
* **Fun Fact:** To install the optical sensors, engineers used a hot-water drill system that carved out 2.5 km deep, 60 cm wide holes into the Antarctic ice sheet in under two days per hole, stringing up PMTs before the holes froze back solid![cite: 1]

[^11]: M. G. Aartsen et al. (IceCube Collaboration), "Measurement of Atmospheric Neutrino Oscillations at 6–56 GeV with IceCube DeepCore," Phys. Rev. Lett. 120, 071801 (2018).[cite: 1]
